Privacy policy

Effective and last updated October 20, 2026.

The short version

Your data goes to the server you run.

This summary helps you understand the policy. The full policy below controls.

Self-hosted by design
Ky apps talk to a server that you, or an operator you choose, runs. Your content and accounts are held there and on your devices. Sharing, sync, backups and integrations can send data to other destinations you or your operator choose.
What we run
Push notification relays that pass a notification from your server to Google or Apple, and this website. The relays see a device token and the notification text. By default that text is generic, with no sender or subject.
No Busnes account
None of our apps create an account with us. We do not sell personal information, show ads, or track you across apps and websites.
Third parties
On Google Play builds, Google handles push delivery, QR-code scanning and optional tips. Apple handles push on Apple devices. Each is listed below with what it receives.
Your choices
KyPost for Android uploads phone contacts only from accounts you select. Notification previews are off by default. Revoking access stops future use; downloaded files, exports and backups may still exist.

3. How Ky apps handle data

Ky server apps are self-hosted. You, or someone you trust such as your employer or IT provider (the operator), runs the server. When this policy says “your server,” it means that operator’s installation. Your server and clients also communicate with mail providers, identity services, push providers and other integrations as described below. We do not receive your mail, contacts, notes, files, calendars, messages or passwords just because you use our software.

The operator decides who has access, how long data is kept, and how it is backed up, so for data held on a server, the operator is responsible. If you are not the operator, ask them for their privacy notice. If you run your own server, you are the operator.

This policy explains what our apps send and to whom, and what we receive when an operator uses the services we run.

4. Services we operate

Push relays (fcm.relay.kysecurity.org for Android, shared by KyPost and KyIdentity; apn.relay.kysecurity.org for KyPost on Apple devices). Google and Apple only deliver notifications to a published app through its publisher’s own credentials. The relays hold those credentials, so a self-hosted server can wake your phone without its own Google or Apple developer account. Your server talks to the relay; the app never does. An operator turns the relays on in the server’s configuration (the example configuration has them on) and can choose “App Pull” delivery instead, which uses no relay.

What the relay receivesWhyWhat it keeps, and for how long
When a server first connects: the server’s public IP address (for IPv6, its /64 network) and its hostnameIssue the server a relay key and limit abuse to one key per addressA key record (key ID, hostname, IP address, creation date) until the key is revoked. No automatic expiry.
For each notification: the device push token, the notification title and text, and a small data payloadDeliver the notification to Google or AppleThe text and payload are held in memory only while being delivered, then discarded. A one-way SHA-256 hash of the push token is stored with the key ID of the server that owns it, so no other server can push to your device. No automatic expiry.
Request metadata (time, server key ID, outcome, Google’s or Apple’s error reason on failure)Run the service, diagnose failures, prevent abuseOperational logs at Cloudflare, kept for up to 7 days. A count of notifications per server key and hostname, kept for 3 months.

What is in a notification. By default, a new-mail notification says only “You have a new email” and carries a message ID, with no sender, subject or keywords. If you turn on notification previews for your account, the sender, subject and keywords are included. A KyPost sign-in approval request carries a challenge ID, a time, and the match number with decoy numbers; with previews on, it also includes the IP address and browser of the sign-in attempt. Messages you receive PGP-encrypted never show a preview. Anything in a notification passes through the relay and through Google or Apple in readable form, so leave previews off if that matters to you.

KyIdentity sign-in approvals. KyIdentity sends KyAuth sign-in approvals for Android through the same Android relay, and for iPhone through an Apple relay of the same design. For iPhones paired with a current version of KyAuth, the relay receives the push token, a fixed alert and an encrypted request it cannot read. For Android with Firebase, and iPhones paired before October 13, 2026, it receives the push token, a device ID, a user ID, a challenge ID, the purpose of the request and its expiry time, unencrypted. It keeps the same kinds of records for the same periods as the KyPost relays.

OpenPGP private keys. Current KyPost uses client-held private keys for end-to-end encryption; server-side private-key generation, import and decryption are retired. A server may retain client-sealed recovery copies and legacy key records for migration. Until a legacy key is migrated, the server keeps a copy it can open for export; migrating removes it. KyPost Server no longer decrypts or signs mail with legacy keys. If you send to someone without a key using the optional server-held secure link, the server can read that message until it is opened or expires (up to 7 days). Ordinary mail that is not end-to-end encrypted is readable by your mail provider and KyPost server. See the KyPost key and migration guide.

On the device. KyPost for Android keeps mail and contacts in an encrypted database, keeps pairing secrets encrypted with keys in the Android Keystore, and excludes all app data from Android cloud backup and device-to-device transfer. Attachments you save go to your Downloads folder, where other apps can read them. With Hostile Location Protection on, mail is kept in memory only.

No Busnes servers. KyPost for Android connects to your KyPost server, to Google services on Google Play and GitHub builds (section 10), to a UnifiedPush distributor if you choose one, and to nothing operated by us. It has no analytics, crash-reporting or advertising SDK.

Things only you can trigger. Remote images in an email are blocked until you tap to show them; showing them contacts the sender’s servers and can tell the sender you opened the message. Scanning someone else’s PGP key QR code fetches their key from the server named in the code, which sees your IP address.

KyPost for iPhone, Mac and Linux keep secrets in the Keychain or the desktop’s Secret Service, and KyPost for Linux keeps an imported PGP key in your GnuPG keyring. KyPost for Linux receives notifications through UnifiedPush, or by checking your server every 90 seconds, and never uses our relay. None of the KyPost apps include analytics or crash reporting.

What KyPost Server contacts. Besides your mail provider, the server checks GitHub hourly for new KyPost Server, KyPost for Linux and Ollama releases, which shows GitHub the server’s IP address. When you send encrypted mail or look up a key, it asks the recipient’s domain (WKD) and then keys.openpgp.org for a public key, which tells them the recipient’s address. Mail classification uses a local model by default; if your operator points it at an outside AI service, that service receives the sender, subject and the first 2,000 characters of each message, after redaction. If your operator enables Cloudflare receiving, incoming mail waits in the operator’s own Cloudflare account until the server collects it, then is deleted. CAPTCHA services are off unless the operator enables Cloudflare Turnstile or Friendly Captcha.

Accounts. Your KyPost account is created and held by your operator’s server, not by us. The app has no sign-up and creates no account with Busnes.app.

KyIdentity stores accounts, devices, sessions, and audit events, including the IP address and browser of each sign-in. Audit events are deleted automatically after 180 days. By default it also contacts Google’s Android attestation revocation list and, for Linux phones, the UBports push service, which receives encrypted payloads only.

Identity sharing and email. KyIdentity sends identity claims to applications you sign in to, and account, group and access changes to provisioning connectors your operator configures. Those applications keep their own records. If the operator configures SMTP delivery, the mail provider handles recipient addresses and verification or recovery messages, including their one-time links. On iPhone and Linux phones, unpairing in KyAuth removes the pairing from the phone only; ask your operator, or use your KyIdentity account page, to remove the device from the server. KyAuth for Android also asks the server to delete it, as described below.

Google services. Only the Google Play and GitHub builds include these; the F-Droid build contains no Google code. See Google’s privacy policy.

  • Firebase Cloud Messaging, only if you pair using Firebase delivery. Google then receives a Firebase installation ID, the FCM token, the app version and basic device information, to deliver sign-in requests. What never leaves your phone. One-time-code secrets and account names, kept in an encrypted vault that opens only with biometrics, screen lock or your optional PIN; your PIN; and biometric data, since KyAuth only learns whether the check succeeded or failed. KyAuth has no backup, export, sync, ads, analytics or crash reporting.

Keeping and deleting data.

  • On your phone: “Unpair” removes the pairing, the device key and the push token. “Reset KyAuth” erases everything. Five wrong PINs erase everything. Uninstalling erases everything.
  • On the server: unpairing asks KyIdentity to delete the device record, on servers that support it. You can also remove the phone on your KyIdentity devices page, or ask your server operator.
  • At Google: under Google’s Firebase privacy and security terms and its ML Kit data disclosure.

Security. All traffic to KyIdentity uses HTTPS only, and KyAuth follows no redirects. KyAuth blocks screenshots and Android backup. KyAuth is not directed to children under 13.

7. Other Ky apps and installation tools

These products keep their application data on the operator’s infrastructure. Their data, encryption and retention differ. Optional identity, document editing, notifications, backups and AI integrations may send information to additional services. Ask your operator which connections are enabled.

Find your product: KyNotes, KyCalendar, KyDrive and Desktop, KyMessages, KyRecovery, KyDNS, kyPulse, KyYard, or installation tools.

KyNotes

Note and attachment content is encrypted in your browser before upload. Your server stores the encrypted content, accounts, notebook access and operational records. Sharing a notebook gives the recipients access to its content. Exported notes and content opened on an authorised device need that device’s protection. Configured recovery destinations receive sealed backups, and a configured blob mirror receives the encrypted note and attachment files. See KyNotes and its documentation.

KyCalendar

Your server stores calendars and event details, such as titles, descriptions, times and locations, together with sharing permissions, accounts and device credentials. Calendar content is not end-to-end encrypted from the server. Members of a shared calendar can read what their permissions allow. CalDAV clients such as Apple Calendar, DAVx5 and Thunderbird download copies to their own devices. An AI agent you connect can read the calendars its token permits and may send event content to its model provider. Calendars you subscribe to are fetched by your server from the feed’s address. Recovery destinations receive sealed backups when configured on SQLite installations; with PostgreSQL the operator’s own database backups hold readable event content. See KyCalendar and its documentation.

KyDrive and KyDrive Desktop

Your KyDrive server holds files, versions, names, sharing permissions, account records and an audit trail. Stored file content and names are encrypted at rest, but the server holds the decryption key and decrypts downloads. This is not end-to-end encryption. Shared-workspace members, people using sharing links and applications with authorised access can receive file content. If document editing is enabled, the configured Euro-Office Document Server processes plaintext documents and previews.

KyDrive Desktop uploads and downloads the workspaces you choose and keeps local files and sync state on Windows, macOS or Linux. Linking sends device details to your server; credentials are stored in the operating system’s keyring. Local synced files are readable on that computer. A revoke that requests a wipe removes synced copies the next time the computer reaches the server; a computer offline for 30 days is never told, so this is not a guaranteed remote wipe. Meanwhile, changes that never uploaded are preserved in the local Unsynced folder. Revocation cannot recall copies someone exported. Server trash, versions, Restic snapshots and KyRecovery capsules have separate retention settings. See KyDrive, the server guide and the desktop guide.

KyMessages

KyMessages manages a Matrix deployment with Synapse, Matrix Authentication Service and Element. The server sees account details, room names, membership, timestamps and other metadata. Element encrypts messages in encrypted rooms, but Synapse does not prevent another client from sending plaintext; do not assume that every stored message is encrypted. Voice and video use the configured call infrastructure, including LiveKit and TURN where enabled. Text-message encryption does not by itself establish how calls are protected.

Federation is closed by default. If the operator permits named servers, room data and metadata can reach their operators and members. An agent invited to a room can decrypt messages it reads and send that content to its configured model; a hosted model takes it off your infrastructure. Closing a room or deleting an account does not erase its history. Permanent room-history deletion does not remove the room’s attachments from the media store. See KyMessages and its current deployment and encryption limits.

KyRecovery

Other Ky products seal backup capsules before depositing them. KyRecovery stores that ciphertext, capsule metadata such as service name, version, size and recovery key ID, custodian names, pairing records and an audit ledger. It does not receive the recovery private key or custodian shares and cannot decrypt a capsule. During the browser key ceremony, the private key and printable shares exist in the ceremony tab and any copies you print or save; keep those copies secure. Retention and any storage destinations are controlled by the operator. Removing a live account does not automatically remove older capsules. See KyRecovery and its recovery and retention guide.

KyDNS

KyDNS processes queried domain names and client network addresses, and stores the service directory, DNS configuration and any DHCP leases it manages. Upstream resolvers receive lookups KyDNS cannot answer locally, together with the resolver host’s network address; encrypted DNS protects the connection, not the query from the upstream provider. The example configuration uses Cloudflare and Quad9. Blocklist downloads contact the configured list provider, which can see the server’s IP address. The operator chooses upstreams, lists, access rules and diagnostic logging. See KyDNS and its configuration guide.

kyPulse

kyPulse polls configured health endpoints and can collect KyYard container facts, application logs and audit activity. These records can include user names, IP addresses, resource names and anything an application writes to its logs. Administrators can inspect collected logs and activity. Configured webhook recipients receive alert details. Removing a source stops future collection but does not erase historical records immediately; retention settings control their lifetime. Sealed recovery backups have their own retention and do not include every collected log or activity row. See kyPulse and its logging and retention guide.

KyYard

KyYard manages the Docker hosts and Kubernetes clusters you connect. It handles endpoint addresses, inventory, deployment configuration, environment values, access credentials, metrics, logs and audit events. People granted management or terminal access can see application data exposed by those privileges. Agents communicate with the configured control plane; deployments, image operations and updates contact the registries and infrastructure providers you use. Pairing kyPulse lets it read selected operational records with a read-only token, and configured recovery destinations receive sealed backups. See KyYard and its access and deployment guide.

KyQuickStart, KyQuickStart AI and Ky Kubernetes

Installation and deployment tools use host names, IP addresses, user names, SSH access and configuration or credentials needed to install the selected services. They contact source repositories, package and container registries, and the DNS, certificate or tunnel providers you configure. Those providers receive the requests needed to deliver their service.

KyQuickStart AI launches the coding agent you choose. Prompts, commands, outputs and files read by that agent can be processed by its model provider under that provider’s terms. Instructions and redaction reduce accidental secret exposure but cannot guarantee that an agent never reads or transmits a secret. Local run reports and the handover sheet can contain sensitive deployment details and passwords. Protect them, and remove them when you no longer need them. Uninstalling an installer does not delete the servers, provider accounts or credentials it created. See the guides for KyQuickStart, KyQuickStart AI. The Ky Kubernetes configuration is not public yet.

8. App permissions

  • Contacts (KyPost for Android): to show your KyPost contacts in the phone’s Contacts app, and, only for phone accounts you choose, to upload contacts to your KyPost server. You can deny it and still use mail.
  • Notifications (KyPost, KyAuth): to tell you about new mail and sign-in requests.
  • Camera: to scan pairing and PGP key QR codes. On Google Play and GitHub builds, Google’s code scanner handles the camera and KyPost does not hold the camera permission. The F-Droid builds, KyPost for iPhone, KyPost for Linux and KyAuth for iPhone ask for the camera directly. Images are not stored or sent.
  • Biometrics (fingerprint or face): to unlock the app and approve sign-ins. Your biometric data never leaves the operating system; the app only learns whether the check passed.
  • Network, background work and start at boot: to sync and receive notifications.

9. Tips and payments

KyPost for Android on Google Play lets you leave an optional tip, either once or as a monthly subscription. Tips unlock nothing; every feature is free. Google Play processes the payment under Google Payments’ privacy notice. We never see your card details, and the app does not link a tip to your email or KyPost account. Google gives us an order record (order ID, product, price, country and status) for accounting and refunds, which we keep as long as tax law requires. Cancel a monthly tip in Google Play under Payments and subscriptions.

KyPost for iPhone offers an optional one-time tip through the App Store, processed by Apple under Apple’s privacy policy; we receive sales reports, not your identity. Tips unlock nothing. KyPost for Mac has no tip jar.

Builds from GitHub and F-Droid, and KyPost for Linux, link to an external Buy Me a Coffee page instead. Anything you give there is handled under Buy Me a Coffee’s own policy.

11. What we do not do

  • We do not sell or rent personal information, or share it for cross-context behavioural advertising.
  • Our apps contain no advertising, and no analytics or crash-reporting SDK. The Google services in section 10 collect their own diagnostics; we do not receive them.
  • We do not track you across other companies’ apps or websites.
  • We do not use your data to train AI models. If you or your operator connects an external model or coding agent, that provider’s terms govern its handling of the content it receives; review them before enabling it.

12. Deleting data and unpairing

  • Unpair KyPost for Android in the app. It tells your server to forget the device, deletes the push token and Firebase installation ID, and deletes mail, contacts, keys and account settings from the phone. Uninstalling also removes app data from the phone, but does not tell the server.
  • Stop contact upload by turning off contact sync or unticking an account. This stops future uploads; contacts already uploaded stay on your KyPost server until you delete them there.
  • Server data (your mail account, contacts, notes, files, sign-in history) is held by your operator. Delete individual items such as messages and contacts in the app or web interface, or ask your operator. KyPost Server can deactivate an account, which revokes its access, but does not yet delete the account’s stored data; the operator removes that from the server.
  • Data we hold. We hold no account for you. Once a device is unpaired, the hashed token at the relay refers to a token that no longer works. Operators can ask us to delete a server’s relay key and its token records, and anyone can ask us to delete correspondence, by writing to privacy@busnes.app. We aim to respond within 30 days.

Copies and backups. Revoking access, unpairing or deleting a live item does not necessarily erase downloaded files, exports, other participants’ copies, log records or backups. Each product’s retention and purge controls apply separately. Ask the operator about backup expiry and any copies held by connected services. KyDrive Desktop may preserve unsent changes locally, and Matrix account deletion can leave room history and media.

13. Security

Encryption depends on the product and configuration. Use HTTPS for remotely accessible web apps and client connections; local development and some DNS or infrastructure protocols have different transports. KyNotes content and encrypted Matrix messages use client-side encryption; KyDrive decrypts files on its server; KyCalendar stores readable event content; KyRecovery holds sealed capsules. Device key storage does not encrypt every local download or export. The relays never write notification text to storage, hold no admin interface, and keep their credentials as encrypted secrets. No system is perfectly secure. Report vulnerabilities to privacy@busnes.app or through the project’s GitHub security page.

14. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct, delete, restrict or receive a copy of personal information; to object to certain processing; to withdraw consent; and to complain to a privacy regulator. We rely on providing the service you asked for, and on our legitimate interest in keeping it secure, to process the limited data described in section 4.

Send requests about data we hold to privacy@busnes.app. For data on a self-hosted server, contact that server’s operator. We may need to verify that a request comes from you or from the server concerned.

15. Children

Our apps and services are not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child’s information reached a service we operate, contact us and we will delete it.

16. This website and correspondence

busnes.app is a static site with no advertising, analytics, cookies, accounts or embedded third-party content. GitHub Pages hosts the site. GitHub receives request metadata, such as your IP address, requested page, referrer, browser and time, to deliver and protect it, under GitHub’s privacy statement. GitHub controls its infrastructure log retention; we do not promise a separate deletion period for those logs. The theme switch stores your light or dark choice in your browser only.

If you email us or contact us through GitHub, we use your name, address and message to reply, provide support, and handle security reports. Email providers and GitHub process this under their own terms. Please do not send passwords, keys or other credentials by email.
