1. Who we are

Busnes.app is a d/b/a operated by Matthew Beacher. In this policy, “Busnes.app,” “we,” and “us” refer to Matthew Beacher doing business as Busnes.app. We publish the Ky apps and operate the services described in section 4. Contact us at privacy@busnes.app.

2. Apps this policy covers

This policy covers every app and service published by Busnes.app:

  • KyPost for Android: Google Play package org.kysecurity.mail. The same app is also published on GitHub as org.kysecurity.mail.github and for F-Droid as org.kysecurity.mail.fdroid.
  • KyPost for Linux (Flatpak com.kysecurity.mail), KyPost for iPhone and KyPost for Mac (bundle ID org.kysecurity.mail, in testing), and the browser client that ships with KyPost Server.
  • KyAuth, the sign-in approver for KyIdentity: Android org.kysecurity.authenticator (GitHub and F-Droid builds), iPhone (in development) and Linux phones (kyauth.busnes, in development).
  • Self-hosted server software: KyPost Server, KyIdentity, KyNotes, KyCalendar, KyDrive, KyMessages, KyRecovery, KyDNS, KyPulse, KyYard, and the KyQuickStart installer.
  • Services we operate: the KyPost and KyIdentity push relays, and this website.

3. How Ky apps handle data

Every Ky app is self-hosted. You, or someone you trust such as your employer or IT provider (the operator), runs the server. When an app sends your data “to the server,” it means that server and no other. We do not receive your mail, contacts, notes, files, calendars, messages or passwords just because you use our software.

The operator decides who has access, how long data is kept, and how it is backed up, so for data held on a server, the operator is responsible. If you are not the operator, ask them for their privacy notice. If you run your own server, you are the operator.

This policy explains what our apps send and to whom, and what we receive when an operator uses the services we run.

4. Services we operate

KyPost push relays (fcm.relay.kysecurity.org for Android, apn.relay.kysecurity.org for Apple devices). Google and Apple only deliver notifications to a published app through its publisher’s own credentials. The relays hold those credentials, so a self-hosted server can wake your phone without its own Google or Apple developer account. Your server talks to the relay; the app never does. An operator turns the relays on in the server’s configuration (the example configuration has them on) and can choose “App Pull” delivery instead, which uses no relay.

What the relay receivesWhyWhat it keeps, and for how long
When a server first connects: the server’s public IP address and its hostnameIssue the server a relay key and limit abuse to one key per addressA key record (key ID, hostname, IP address, creation date) until the key is revoked. No automatic expiry.

A one-way SHA-256 hash of the push token is stored with the key ID of the server that owns it, so no other server can push to your device. No automatic expiry.
Request metadata (time, server key ID, outcome, Google’s or Apple’s error reason on failure)Run the service, diagnose failures, prevent abuseOperational logs at Cloudflare, kept for up to 7 days. A count of notifications per server key and hostname, kept for 3 months.

What is in a notification. By default, a new-mail notification says only “You have a new email” and carries a message ID, with no sender, subject or keywords. If you turn on notification previews for your account, the sender, subject and keywords are included. A sign-in approval request carries a challenge ID, a time and a two-digit match number; with previews on, it also includes the IP address and browser of the sign-in attempt. Messages you receive PGP-encrypted never show a preview. Anything in a notification passes through the relay and through Google or Apple in readable form, so leave previews off if that matters to you.

KyIdentity push relay. KyIdentity uses a relay of the same design to send KyAuth sign-in approvals. It receives the push token, a device ID, a user ID, a challenge ID and the purpose of the request, and keeps the same kinds of records for the same periods as the KyPost relays.

On the device. KyPost for Android keeps mail and contacts in an encrypted database, keeps pairing secrets encrypted with keys in the Android Keystore, and excludes all app data from Android cloud backup and device-to-device transfer. Attachments you save go to your Downloads folder, where other apps can read them. With Hostile Location Protection on, mail is kept in memory only.

No Busnes servers. KyPost for Android connects to your KyPost server, to Google services on Google Play and GitHub builds (section 10), and to nothing operated by us. It has no analytics, crash-reporting or advertising SDK.

Things only you can trigger. Remote images in an email are blocked until you tap to show them; showing them contacts the sender’s servers and can tell the sender you opened the message. Scanning someone else’s PGP key QR code fetches their key from the server named in the code, which sees your IP address.

KyPost for iPhone, Mac and Linux keep secrets in the Keychain or the desktop’s Secret Service. KyPost for Linux receives notifications through UnifiedPush and never uses our relay. None of the KyPost apps include analytics or crash reporting.

What KyPost Server contacts. Besides your mail provider, the server checks GitHub hourly for new KyPost and Ollama releases, which shows GitHub the server’s IP address. When you send encrypted mail or look up a key, it asks the recipient’s domain (WKD) and then keys.openpgp.org for a public key, which tells them the recipient’s address. Mail classification uses a local model by default; if your operator points it at an outside AI service, that service receives the sender, subject and up to 2 KB of each message. If your operator enables Cloudflare receiving, incoming mail waits in the operator’s own Cloudflare account until the server collects it, then is deleted. CAPTCHA services are off unless the operator enables Cloudflare Turnstile or Friendly Captcha.

Accounts. Your KyPost account is created and held by your operator’s server, not by us. The app has no sign-up and creates no account with Busnes.app.

KyIdentity is a self-hosted sign-in service. KyAuth is the phone app that approves sign-ins and generates one-time codes.

KyIdentity stores accounts, devices, sessions, and audit events, including the IP address and browser of each sign-in. Audit events are deleted automatically after 180 days. By default it also contacts Google’s Android attestation revocation list and, for Linux phones, the UBports push service, which receives encrypted payloads only.

Unpairing in KyAuth removes the pairing from the phone only; ask your operator, or use your KyIdentity account page, to remove the device from the server.

7. Other Ky server apps

KyNotes, KyCalendar, KyDrive, KyMessages, KyRecovery, KyDNS, KyPulse and KyYard are server software with a browser interface. None of them ships a phone app or contacts a Busnes.app service by default. Their browser interfaces load no third-party scripts and use cookies only to keep you signed in and protect forms, plus local storage for your light or dark theme.

  • KyNotes encrypts notes and attachments in your browser before they reach the server.
  • KyMessages keeps chats on your server. Federation with other chat servers is off unless the operator enables it, and push notifications carry no message content.
  • KyRecovery stores encrypted backups sent by other Ky apps and copies them only to destinations the operator chooses.
  • KyDNS by default forwards DNS lookups it cannot answer to Cloudflare and Quad9 over encrypted DNS, and downloads a public blocklist from GitHub once a day. The operator can change both.
  • KyPulse sends alerts only to webhooks the operator configures.
  • KyYard contacts container registries only when the operator checks for updates or sets an update policy.
  • KyQuickStart downloads container images and, only if the operator chooses a tunnel, configures Cloudflare Tunnel or frp.

Accounts in these apps are created and deleted by the operator, usually through KyIdentity.

8. App permissions

  • Contacts (KyPost for Android): to show your KyPost contacts in the phone’s Contacts app, and, only for phone accounts you choose, to upload contacts to your KyPost server. You can deny it and still use mail.
  • Notifications (KyPost, KyAuth): to tell you about new mail and sign-in requests.
  • Camera: to scan pairing and PGP key QR codes. On Google Play and GitHub builds, Google’s code scanner handles the camera and KyPost does not hold the camera permission. The F-Droid builds and KyAuth for iPhone ask for the camera directly. Images are not stored or sent.
  • Biometrics (fingerprint or face): to unlock the app and approve sign-ins. Your biometric data never leaves the operating system; the app only learns whether the check passed.
  • Network, background work and start at boot: to sync and receive notifications.

9. Tips and payments

KyPost for Android on Google Play lets you leave an optional tip, either once or as a monthly subscription. Tips unlock nothing; every feature is free. Google Play processes the payment under Google Payments’ privacy notice. We never see your card details, and the app does not link a tip to your email or KyPost account. Google gives us an order record (order ID, product, price, country and status) for accounting and refunds, which we keep as long as tax law requires. Cancel a monthly tip in Google Play under Payments and subscriptions.

KyPost for iPhone and Mac offer the same optional tips through the App Store, processed by Apple under Apple’s privacy policy; we receive sales reports, not your identity.

Builds from GitHub and F-Droid, and KyPost for Linux, link to an external Buy Me a Coffee page instead. Anything you give there is handled under Buy Me a Coffee’s own policy.

11. What we do not do

  • We do not sell or rent personal information, or share it for cross-context behavioural advertising.
  • Our apps contain no advertising, and no analytics or crash-reporting SDK. The Google services in section 10 collect their own diagnostics; we do not receive them.
  • We do not track you across other companies’ apps or websites.
  • We do not use your data to train AI models. KyPost’s mail classifier runs on the model your operator configures, not on a service of ours.

12. Deleting data and unpairing

  • Unpair KyPost for Android in the app. It tells your server to forget the device, deletes the push token and Firebase installation ID, and deletes mail, contacts, keys and settings from the phone. Uninstalling also removes app data from the phone, but does not tell the server.
  • Stop contact upload by turning off contact sync or unticking an account. This stops future uploads; contacts already uploaded stay on your KyPost server until you delete them there.
  • Server data (your mail account, contacts, notes, files, sign-in history) is held by your operator. Delete individual items such as messages and contacts in the app or web interface, or ask your operator. KyPost Server can deactivate an account, which revokes its access, but does not yet delete the account’s stored data; the operator removes that from the server.
  • Data we hold. We hold no account for you. Once a device is unpaired, the hashed token at the relay refers to a token that no longer works. Operators can ask us to delete a server’s relay key and its token records, and anyone can ask us to delete correspondence, by writing to privacy@busnes.app. We aim to respond within 30 days.

13. Security

Our apps connect only over HTTPS, encrypt stored data on the device, and keep keys in the operating system’s secure storage. The relays never write notification text to storage, hold no admin interface, and keep their credentials as encrypted secrets. No system is perfectly secure. Report vulnerabilities to privacy@busnes.app or through the project’s GitHub security page.

14. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct, delete, restrict or receive a copy of personal information; to object to certain processing; to withdraw consent; and to complain to a privacy regulator. We rely on providing the service you asked for, and on our legitimate interest in keeping it secure, to process the limited data described in section 4.

Send requests about data we hold to privacy@busnes.app. For data on a self-hosted server, contact that server’s operator. We may need to verify that a request comes from you or from the server concerned.

15. Children

Our apps and services are not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child’s information reached a service we operate, contact us and we will delete it.

16. This website and correspondence

busnes.app is a static site with no advertising, analytics, cookies, accounts or embedded third-party content. Its host receives ordinary request logs (IP address, requested page, referrer, browser and time) to deliver and protect the site. The theme switch stores your light or dark choice in your browser only.

If you email us or contact us through GitHub, we use your name, address and message to reply, provide support, and handle security reports. Email providers and GitHub process this under their own terms. Please do not send passwords, keys or other credentials by email.

17. Changes to this policy

We post changes here and update the date at the top. If a change materially affects what our apps send or what our services keep, we will also announce it in the affected apps’ release notes before it takes effect.
